E. the branch cut of s^(−α) on the negative real axis of the Laplace plane (s = σ + iω, σ < 0)
   IEEE keeps the side in the sign of a zero (Kahan 1987):  (−1 + 0i)^(−α) = -0.8090169943749476 - 0.587785252292473im   (−1 − 0i)^(−α) = -0.8090169943749476 + 0.587785252292473im
   total arithmetic has one zero: (−1)^(−α) = 1.0∠-0.8π  (principal: arg = π)
   the sides are values, not a sign bit:  (−1 + iε)^(−α) = 1.0∠-0.8π⟦≥∠?⟧   (−1 − iε)^(−α) = 1.0∠-0.8π⟦≥∠?⟧
   … but the polar form stores arg/π ∈ (−1, 1] in a Float64: (−1 − iε) has arg/π = −1 + 7e-309, which rounds to −1 and wraps to +1:
       arg(−1 + iε)/π = 1.0   arg(−1 − iε)/π = 1.0   flags 6 / 6
   the direction ε carries survives as a *value* only while |Im/Re| ≳ 1e-16 — below that the polar representation, not the
   arithmetic, forgets the side.  Kahan's sign bit is a 1-bit answer to a question the polar form cannot even ask;
   a Cartesian TotComplex (re, im each totalized) would keep im = −MIN⟦≤⟧ and answer it.  Recorded as a limitation.
